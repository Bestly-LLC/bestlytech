# Sell Amazon Business — Field Sales Playbook

Source: "Phase 1 AB" training packet, Thrive LA (a Cydcor-affiliate sales org), field rep training material for the "Connect Me Better" (CMB) Amazon Business partner program. Captured from Jared Best's training packet, 2025.

## What this is

A door-to-door / walk-in B2B outside sales playbook for reps who visit small businesses in person, help them set up a free Amazon Business account, and place their first order (with an in-person "exclusive discount"). The company running the program (Connect Me Better) is a 3rd-party partner of Amazon Business, not Amazon itself.

## Company standards & values (Thrive LA)

Core values: Integrity, Vision, Growth, Unity.

- Weekly sales target: activate a minimum of 10 clients per week.
- Enrollment spend target: aim for 70% of enrollments to be above the $300 spend amount.
- Dress code: business professional in office, business casual in the field.

### Entry-level Account Executive expectations
1. Commitment — show up on time (8:00 AM) or early (7:45 AM). Tardy policy: conversation on 1st, sent home on 2nd, terminated on 3rd (voluntary termination).
2. Commitment — don't miss a day of training in first 2 weeks unless management informed.
3. Commitment — time-off requests need 2 weeks' notice via email to manager, assistant manager, and leader.
4. Integrity — be upfront and honest with your coach/manager.
5. Initiative — take initiative to learn the business, sales, and program; be an aggressive student.
6. Student Mentality — learn the pitch, it's proven; follow the system (use the 4s, 5s, 8s below). Don't reinvent the wheel.
7. Commitment — don't quit from frustration/discouragement in the first three weeks.

## The 4 Factors of Impulse

1. **Jones Effect** — wanting what others have. Name-drop and connect to the local community (people want to work with people who are part of their community).
   - Verbal: Name Dropping ("I was just with Mary next door at State Farm..."), Industry Jones ("I'm doing a special pricing campaign for medical offices..."), Community Jones (local knowledge/rapport).
   - Non-verbal: show apps of prior orders placed that day nearby, show business cards of current customers in the territory, point toward customers when name-dropping.
2. **Indifference** — "opposite of a salesperson." Genuinely care about the customer without being emotionally attached to their decision; trust the Law of Averages that someone today will say yes.
   - Verbal: "I'm here to speak with the person in charge of ordering office supplies... probably your favorite job, right?" / "I'm definitely not here to get y'all a bunch of stuff you don't need..." / "That's ok, you don't HAVE to order today... it's completely up to you."
   - Non-verbal: shrugging shoulders, stepping back with palms up on objections.
3. **Fear of Loss** — "Today." Fear of missing out on a deal — "we are walking, talking coupons." People can always become Amazon Business customers on their own, but they won't have access to your specials.
   - Verbal: "I'm in town for the day..." / "I'd hate for you guys to miss out on these savings..." / "While you have me here today..." / "To get these special prices, the first order has to go through me..." / "I really don't know when they will have me out here again..."
   - Non-verbal: taking back materials after the presentation, start packing up, slowly pace backward toward the door while speaking.
4. **Sense of Urgency** — "It will only take a few minutes — let's do this today!" Be quick.
   - Verbal: "I'm just stopping in to quickly introduce myself... and then I've got to run." / "I know you're busy; it takes 30 seconds to give you a quick quote." / "I'll do the work for you, that way you won't miss a beat!" / "I'll cut to the chase..."
   - Non-verbal: increased body movement (feet pacing, hand gestures — hands never in pockets), take the business card and start filling out the order form rather than asking for it, glance at watch/clock.

## The 5 Steps to a Sale / Conversation (pitch structure)

1. **Introduction** — Establish Rapport
   - SEE Factor: Smile, Eye Contact, Enthusiasm
   - Break the ice
   - CPR — create a personal relationship
2. **Short Story** — Ask Questions to Qualify
   - Reduce skepticism, build impulse
   - Qualify buyer: Decision Maker?, Current Supplier?, 2+ employees?
   - Qualify concept: why you're there and what you're doing
3. **Presentation** — Establish a Need
   - Price compare, sizzle the deal
   - Keep it Short & Simple (K.I.S.S.)
   - Paint the picture
4. **Close** — Link the Need with the Best Solution
   - Take control, assume the sale, close with confidence and 1 item
5. **Rehash** — Build and Preserve the Sale
   - Suggest more options, increase your profit
   - Remember: everyone has another sale hidden

## CMB Amazon Pitch (verbatim script)

**1. Introduction:**
"Hello!!! How are you doing today!? (Engage in conversation) My name is ___, I'm here with Connect Me Better and Amazon Business, I want to say hi to the person who orders the business supplies?"

**2. Short Story:**
"Awesome! Are you familiar with Amazon Business? Amazon business is the same as the regular account, but the items on the business account are discounted. Amazon has seen that some people still use a personal account to order for the business, so Amazon partnered with Connect Me Better so that I can come by in person to help set up a free business account and give you access to the business discounts."
"Here I can just show you what it looks like real quick" (show business savings)

**3. Presentation:**
"My job is really simple, it's to help you place your first order on Amazon business and activate your account."
- "What's your most frequent expense?"
- "Where do you usually get it from?" (price comparison vs. other companies only)
  - "How often do you get it ___?"
  - "When was the last time that you got it ___?"
  - "Since I am here today, I can make the price ___" (show new total on a calculator)
- Fallback if they don't answer: "What's typically your biggest expense?" (ink/toner), or use the power of suggestion/observation.

**4. Transition to Close (why you are there):**
"My job is really simple, it's to help you walk through the steps to create your free business account and help you place your first order with my discount. All I need is an email address."

Take control and assume the sale:
- "So in order to give you access:"
- "Since you order ___, let's get backups since you'll be using it anyway"
- "Prime gets free shipping and extra offers"
- "Just hop on to amazon.com/register" (point to computer)
- ... break eye contact, and be quiet

## Working your territory correctly

"The will to win is important, but the will to prepare is vital." — John Wooden.

### Target zones (weekly)
- 50–60 businesses
- 30 contacts
- 6–8 decision makers
- 4–5 presentations
- 2–3 new accounts ($300+ avg order size)
- DPR (daily production rate) target: $200

### Field prep document
Before leaving the office, re-write names/notes from tracking sheets. Use past tracking sheets, notes, sales summary sheets, and pipeline to plan:
1. **FARM** — who to revisit for a 2nd order
2. **5-Day Follow-up** — who to revisit to confirm order arrival / sell Amazon Plus
3. **Call Backs** — who you must go back to, appointment window (0–5), mark the time
4. **Re-loops** — who you want to go back to (0–15)
5. **Fresh doors** — have enough fresh doors planned (50+)

### How to structure your day
Weekly goal = 10 new enrollments / 50% at $300+.

- **1st Quarter (10:00am–12:00pm) — Account Management.** Goal: set up residual income. Farming (revisit past-4-weeks customers) + 5-Day Revisits (confirm delivery, set expectation to order, get on Amazon).
- **2nd Quarter (12:00–1:30pm).** Goal: Re-loops — 2nd/3rd loops on doors with no decision-maker contact yet.
- **2nd Half (1:30–5:00pm).** Goal: Fresh Door Blitz — Hunting. Set up 30+ new doors for new accounts. ("20 doors by 1pm" is the ideal pace before this block.)

### Working a territory — loop system
- **Preparation:** have a plan of attack before leaving the office.
- **Execution:** start top-left corner, work every business without skipping any, don't prejudge. Load each lead and log the outcome (MyT) as you go.
- If the customer qualifies: (1) make a sale, or (2) "butter up" the territory (if you hear 2 no's) and return in 2 weeks with your list.
- If unqualified, hand off/disposition and move on.

**Loop 1:** Get information — ask the Fab 5, or sell on impulse. Take all notes in MyT.
**Loop 2 (after 10 working days):** Go back to the start; skip unqualified doors, pitch qualified customers you "buttered up," ask for them by name, check on delivery, build the relationship, encourage a 2nd order.
**Loop 3:** Repeat loop 1's process; track law of averages each pass. After completing an area a 3rd time, let it "breathe" for 2–3 months and move to a new territory. Loop 3 is for "real callbacks," not "crawl backs."

**Territory sizing:** every 600 businesses should last a rep 1 month at 10% saturation (≈60 customers). Re-looping a section 3 times is how a territory lasts and hits that number. Visit an undecided business 3 times before letting the door rest; a definite "no" also gets revisited 3 times before leaving the territory for good.

**A sample 5-day territory cadence:** Day 1 = doors 1–60. Day 2 = re-loop 1–60 (skip definite no's / seen-DM) + fresh doors 61–90. Day 3 = re-loop 1–90 + fresh 91–120. Day 4 = re-loop 60–120 + fresh 120–150. Day 5 = re-loop 90–120 + fresh 150–190. Once fresh doors are done, re-loop the entire territory one last time.

**The Fab 5** — 5 questions to answer at each door for effective re-loops:
1. Who did you speak with?
2. Who do you need to speak with to make decisions?
3. Will they be back later that day or tomorrow? Morning or afternoon?
4. Where do they currently shop for their office supplies?
5. What supplies do they go through the most?

**Loop system (short form):** Loop 1 = get the decision-maker's name or make a sale. Loop 2 = appointments, revisits, finding the decision maker. Loop 3 = final yes or no.

**Retention/first follow-up (5 days after the sale):** confirm the order arrived and is correct. This is about service, not pressure — don't push another order unless they ask. If the order isn't there yet, build impulse back up and get them on the phone with Amazon to check status (customer pre-pays). If it is there, have the "commitment talk" and plant the seed for the next order: "I'm sure you'll be running low on some of these things by then... order away in the meantime... if you need help though, I will be back on the sales flyer expiration date... assume the 2nd sale."

## Law of Averages

"We use the Law of Averages to focus on sales." Rolling a pair of 6s (two dice) = making a sale. You don't know when it'll happen, but the more you throw the dice, the more chances you get. Throwing the dice is a **process**; a pair of 6s is a **result**.

Tips: there is always a sale in a day; maintain your attitude toward the "no's" — you're one step closer to a "yes"; work your territory correctly and don't prejudge; stay excited and enthusiastic even without a sale yet.

## Building rapport (morning activity)

Communication breakdown: 7% words, 38% how you say it (tone/pace/volume), 55% non-verbal (eye contact, posture, gestures, facial expression).

- **Be engaging** — SEE Factors (Smile, Eye contact, Enthusiasm/Energy); name exchange + simple non-Amazon question to set a comfortable tone ("Very nice to meet you. How are you today?").
- **Verbal/non-verbal mirroring** — respond in the style most desirable to the recipient; mirror posture, eye contact, hand use, pace/tone if natural.
- **Relationship building** — talk about neighborhood/city news, recent or upcoming holidays, and **FORDS**: Family, Occupation, Recreation, Dreams, School/Sports.
- **Power of observation** — notice pictures of family/pets/hobbies, plaques, books, sports memorabilia, flashy electronics/furniture, gifts — use them as conversation hooks.

## Understanding & overcoming objections

Tips: don't rebut an objection until you actually get one (don't pre-empt negatives that don't exist yet — overcomplicates the pitch). Use SEE factors (AIR — smile) while responding. Never interrupt; listen for hotspots/needs. Overturn most objections in the CLOSE phase, after you've painted the picture. After overcoming an objection, always go back to the pitch.

### The 5 main objections and why reps get them
1. **"Leave a business card/catalog"** — unclear transition to close, DM lazy/not engaged, rep lacks urgency.
2. **"We're too small"** — DM thinks there's a contract/membership fee, thinks the rep wants lots of future calls/visits, or thinks great prices mean pressure to buy in bulk.
3. **"Satisfied with current supplier"** — savings aren't the DM's priority (convenience/relationship matters more), they see you as a salesman rather than a consultant, or switching feels like too big a decision (rep needs to make it low-pressure — sub-vendor, no-brainer).
4. **"Too busy/don't have time"** — DM thinks signing up is a long process, rep doesn't take enough control ("I just need the card"), rep lacks body-movement/urgency.
5. **"All stocked up/just ordered"** — DM is lazy and rep needs to take more control; DM has tunnel vision (suggest more than pens/paper); DM is genuinely well-stocked and on top of pricing (rep needs to show value); or "I need to run this by / check with ___" — decision feels too big (rep needs to simplify, make it low-pressure).

### Overcoming artificial/early objections — The Three Rs
- **Repeat** — smile and acknowledge: "Ah, that makes sense" (listen).
- **Reassure** — relate that it's simpler/easier than they think: "most of my customers..." (Jones effect).
- **Resume** — pick the pitch back up where you left off.

**Reassure with The Three Ts:** "That's actually why I am here." / "That's actually why people love/use Amazon." / "That's actually the great thing/best part about Amazon."

**Artificial objections** (heard right at the door, from a misunderstanding of why you're there): "I'm busy," "I'm all set," "I'm not interested," "I'm not the one who handles that," "No soliciting," "We just switched, we're fine." Response for all: "I understand (pause). This will only take a moment." Then resume the pitch.

### Early vs. late objection rebuttals (by type)

| Objection | Early response | Late response |
|---|---|---|
| Too small | "We have no minimums so you aren't required to place a monthly/yearly order." / "You have to buy it anyway, why not save on it?" / "Let me just give you a quick quote to see if it's a good fit." / Resume. | "You're going to have to buy this anyway; why not have access so you can use it whenever you want." / "So like I said, in order to give you access we have to start you off with something simple like ___." |
| Busy | If busy, compliment before they say it; if they say it, note it only takes 2 mins; "I just want to leave you with a quick quote"; "Is that your main printer?" | "Literally only takes me 2 mins to set you up." / "All I'll do is give you a quick user name and password." / "So when you're busy in the future you can hop on and save time." / "Or do a phone order — I'll do all the work for you." |
| Stocked up | "Not trying to sell you anything, just want to give you a quick quote so in the future you can save." / Resume. | "Not trying to turn you into a warehouse, I just want to give you access so in the future you can save on this ___." |
| Biz card / email | "It's actually a lot easier than that, I just want to give you a quick quote to see if it's a good fit." | "It's actually a lot easier than that, I'm just a matchmaker — my job is just to give you access, so in the future you can use us whenever you want." |
| Local (already have a local supplier) | "We actually have no contracts so you can keep using ___, just use us on the things I can help you save on, let me just give you a quick quote to see if it's a good fit." | "We have no contracts, so you can keep using ___. Just use us on things we can help you save on, like ink and paper, and use them on everything else." |

**Situational Q&A**
1. Establish Rapport — *Person seems very busy?* Point out the obvious, use as icebreaker: "Wow, you guys are really busy — are you always this busy?"
2. Qualifying Questions — *Decision maker not in?* Use the Fab 5, take notes: name, when they'll be in, mornings/afternoons, retail or delivery, current supplier.
   *Decision maker in the back?* Use the laminate + "Cali-Roll" technique: "Here! Give them this and let them know it's John from Amazon and I just need 2 quick seconds! (smile)... Thanks (walk away)."
   *Business doesn't use "office supplies"?* Ask questions: "Do you guys ever go to Wal-Mart, Sam's for anything at all? What do you guys go for?"
3. Establish a Need — *Customer says they're "corporate"?* They'll already have a system in place — ask how it works: "So how does it work? When your guys are in need of something, where do you guys go?"
4. Link the Need with best Solution — *Their hot spot isn't ink, or you can't compete on ink price?* Use **SSRRSS**: Suggest other items, Rebuild relationship, Re-impulse, Suggest industry-specific items, Suggest Hail Mary items.
   *Customer appears mad?* Politely leave — a bad mood is usually about their day, not you; leave respectfully and they (or another rep) may get a second chance.
5. Close and preserve the Sale — *Customer backs out and you have to rip the application?* That's fine — you took control and got through the close. Not everyone commits due to timing; keep working the Law of Averages and someone will say yes.

## Frequently encountered issues + solutions (enrollment)

**Email already associated with an Amazon account:** add "+1" to an existing Gmail address (only works on Gmail-hosted domains — companyname+1@gmail.com); use a different email on the business domain; use a different unassociated email; or have the customer create a new (personal-use) email so they don't get charged for a "business Gmail."

**Phone number already associated with an Amazon account** (only relevant when the OTP captcha appears): close all Amazon browser tabs and restart registration to avoid the OTP puzzle; if it recurs, use a personal Google Voice number to receive the OTP by text (delete it from account settings after enrollment and replace with the customer's real number); or use an alternate number owned by the DM.

**24–48 hour verification hold:** usually caused by having a personal Amazon account open in another tab, or a mistake during registration (e.g., personal address used instead of business address). Prevent by closing all other Amazon tabs first, confirming details on every page before proceeding, and confirming the business address is correct.

**Customer locked out of their Amazon account** (unusual activity flag): to regain access, check email/text for an "Account on hold" notification, sign in, and complete the form with required attachments.

**Discount code not applying:** payment method must be entered *before* the discount code; payment method must be set to "only me" when entering the code.

**Payment declined:** re-enter card details, or remove and re-add the same card; if it persists, use a different card number.

**General enrollment tips:** avoid cell-phone registrations — always use desktop when possible; if stuck, try a different email and restart registration.

## Building the order for max retention

1. Use expressions like "Most of my customers prefer..." and "I strongly suggest..." — said with confidence, you sound like a consultant and customers trust you.
2. High/low pitching — always close on 1 item before rehashing (suggesting other items); don't pitch 2 expensive items in a row; alternate high-dollar and low-dollar items; leave quantities blank on the note pad until checkout.
3–4. Example framing: "So on these toners... the most I'm allowed to do with the 25% off is a platinum pack of 20..." / "So these post-its are $9.99 a dozen, but there's a limit of 2 per customer, so do you want to go ahead and get 2?"
5. Check the "Industry Rehash sheet" before walking in to build a small arsenal of the specific items that business uses.
6. Suggest general items (paper, ink/toner, etc.).
7. Suggest industry-specific items.
8. Rebuild relationship (use FORDS).
9. Re-explain — give a short version of the transition to close.
10. Suggest something they'll use in the next several months.
11. Suggest Hail Mary items.
12. Use dead time effectively (e.g., waiting room, waiting for the DM to finish a call).
13. Look around the office to see what products they use — start a scratch list while filling out the form.
14. Look up their toner and check the yield next to the price — this shows exact usage/frequency and helps with rehash; also ask their monthly paper usage.
15. Look for competitor info: catalogs, order forms, product packaging.

### The 3 steps of building an order
1. **Shopping mode** — "No one likes to be sold, everyone likes to buy." The site is built to encourage shopping — guide the customer into the shopping process.
2. **Gather all printers — toner, toner, toner. Get every machine.** Ask deeper questions: how many backups do you like to keep? How many do you have on hand? Let's check your ink level. How often do you go through ink, which is busiest? When you buy toners, do you buy in multiples? Brand name or cheapest? Do you like your printer? Mentality: it doesn't make sense to save a customer money on one toner and not another — find all their printers and mention most customers save the most on ink/toner with the startup discount.
3. **Pitch in packs/multiples and get quantities at the end.** Pitch price breaks (e.g., "There's a price break at 4 boxes, but I can do more if you like" — 20/10/5 packs; roughly 1-in-5 will choose the top tier, 1-in-5 will insist on just 1, 3-in-5 will get 2). Frame the delivery around savings, not cost: "With that toner where we're saving you $15, the most I could do is our platinum 20-pack — that would last about a year and a half and save you $300. That might be too much for your size business. We also have our executive 10-pack, which is what most businesses your size get — that saves you $150. We also have a little small-business 5-pack. What would you prefer?" Talk about savings, not cost — how much they'll spend can be scary, how much they could save is fear of loss. Pitch high then low, watch them buy, use bullets throughout the rehash like free shipping. Always offer the least discount possible when closing — leave room to incentivize a bigger order later.

Mentality: people go to Sam's/Costco to buy in bulk because it saves more — the same logic applies here. Note: you must take control and suggest with confidence for rehash to work. It's normal for a business to spend $300–$1500+ on a single order.

## The BOLT system (Bulls, Owls, Lambs, Tigers) — personality typing

Identify which animal type a decision-maker is to adapt your pitch.

**BULL** — seeks to control the negotiation/situation, likes the bottom line ("get to the point," not A-to-Z, just Z), fast to decide/fast-paced, prestige and status matter more than security, likes challenges, results-oriented, business first then social.
- First 30 seconds: notice how they dress/how their business looks, compliment as icebreaker/CPR, keep it quick — time = money.
- Presentation: make them feel they're taking advantage of something, they're fast to decide and like prestige, get to the point.
- Mention: business-specific features, discounts, business analytics, access to a vast marketplace.

**OWL** — very analytical, detail-oriented, researcher; takes time to decide, slow-paced; no mistakes (theirs or yours); dislikes over-excitement/emotionalism; security matters more than prestige/status; business first then social.
- First 30 seconds: speak slow, don't show too much excitement, be a serious professional, keep eye contact the whole time you're speaking, explain clearly why you're there.
- Presentation: they like to research — don't push or you'll lose them; ask what questions they have after the presentation. Jones Effect works well: "A lot of people in the area were having issues with..." Indifference also works well.
- Mention: business-specific features, discounts, business analytics, access to a vast marketplace.

**LAMB** — be their friend, give direction, show support; slow to decide, often changes mind (high buyer's remorse); very emotional, avoids conflict; security more important than prestige/status; social first then business.
- First 30 seconds: be super friendly, ask questions, mention things you can talk about a bit before pitching.
- Presentation: likes to please people — make them feel secure ("I will take care of this for you"); mention how the "share loop" can be unsafe and how you fix that; use the Jones Effect to build security.
- Mention: business-specific features, discounts, business analytics, access to a vast marketplace.

**TIGER** — not into details, prefers others handle them; fast to decide, "messy messy," fast-paced, often late for appointments; loves recognition, emotional, likes excitement; prestige/status matter more than security; likes a challenge; social relationships matter; social first then business, impulsive/excitable.
- First 30 seconds: use your energy to excite them, they'll love a compliment, be genuine.
